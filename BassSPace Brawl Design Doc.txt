Game Design Brief — YARD BRAWL
Working Title

BassSpace Brawl

Genre

Physics-based multiplayer arena brawler / roguelite PvP

Players

2–8 players

Platform

PC / Steam

Core Pitch

Yard Brawl is a chaotic multiplayer physics shooter where 2–8 players fight inside a shared 3D house and backyard using shove-powered weapons, throwable objects, environmental hazards, and absurd player upgrades.

The game combines the authoritative physics sandbox and proximity-voice multiplayer of the existing browser starter with the round-based escalation and build-crafting philosophy of ROUNDS.

Every match starts relatively simple.

Then somebody gets a card that makes their shove gun launch players across the yard.

Someone else gets explosive balls.

Another player becomes absurdly fast.

Someone stacks bouncing projectiles with homing effects.

By the final rounds, the house should look like it has been through a minor natural disaster.

1. Core Gameplay Loop

The fundamental loop is:

FIGHT → WIN → CHOOSE UPGRADE → FIGHT AGAIN → STACK UPGRADES → FINAL BATTLE

Each match consists of a series of short rounds.

Before the match

Players spawn into a shared house/yard lobby.

They can:

Throw objects
Pick up furniture
Mess with doors
Bounce balls around
Test their weapons
Talk through proximity voice
Generally cause problems

When everyone is ready, the arena locks down and the round begins.

2. The Round Structure

Each round should take approximately:

30–90 seconds

A round ends when:

Only one player remains, or
A team wins, depending on game mode.

The winner earns progression toward the next upgrade.

After each round

The arena briefly enters a Build Phase.

Players receive a selection of randomized upgrade cards.

For example:

BIG YEET
Your shove force is increased by 75%.

or:

RUBBER BULLET
Projectiles bounce twice before disappearing.

or:

ANGRY BALL
Thrown balls accelerate toward nearby players.

or:

DOOR SLAMMER
Sliding doors launch players they hit.

Players select one upgrade and immediately return to combat.

3. The ROUNDS Inspiration

The important part isn't directly copying ROUNDS.

Instead, take its fundamental design philosophy:

Simple base weapon + ridiculous modifiers

The player's basic weapon should initially be extremely simple.

For example:

Shove Gun

Instead of firing conventional bullets, it launches an extremely powerful physics impulse.

At the beginning:

PEW → shove

After several upgrades:

PEW → bouncing projectile → explosion → chain reaction → player flies through window → hits ball → ball hits another player → screaming

The weapon itself remains recognizable while the modifiers dramatically change how it behaves.

4. Weapon System

The game's signature weapon is the Shove Gun.

It doesn't primarily deal damage.

It applies force.

This makes the existing physics system the game's primary combat mechanic.

Base Weapon

Shove Gun

Short/medium range
Strong knockback
Limited ammunition
Reload time
Physics-based projectile
Can interact with props
Can knock players through doors/windows
Can launch objects

The player doesn't necessarily kill opponents by reducing HP to zero.

Instead, the primary objective is:

GET THEM OUT OF THE ARENA.

6. The Environment Is a Weapon

One of the game's biggest differentiators should be:

If it moves, it can probably hurt somebody.

Players can interact with:

Chairs
Tables
Trash cans
Basketballs
Boxes
Bottles
Buckets
Toys
Furniture
Doors
Balls
Random household junk

A player might grab a chair and throw it.

Another player might shove the chair.

The chair hits a door.

The door opens.

The door hits somebody.

That player falls into the pool.

That's the kind of emergent gameplay the game should encourage.

7. Upgrade System

Upgrades should modify physics, weapons, movement, projectiles, and environmental interaction.

They should also stack.

Projectile Upgrades

BOUNCY

Projectiles bounce off surfaces.

TRIPLE SHOT

Fire three projectiles.

RICOCHET

Projectiles automatically change direction after hitting something.

HOMING

Projectiles slightly track nearby players.

BIG BALL

Projectile size increased dramatically.

EXPLOSIVE

Projectiles create a powerful physics impulse on impact.

Knockback Upgrades

YEET

+50% knockback.

MEGA YEET

+100% knockback.

WALL BONK

Hitting a wall creates another knockback pulse.

CHAIN REACTION

Knocking one player into another transfers some of the force.

Movement Upgrades

BUNNY HOP

Gain additional air control.

SUPER JUMP

Massively increased jump force.

AIR DASH

Gain an additional midair dash.

SLIPPERY

Players retain horizontal momentum longer.

ROCKET LEGS

Jumping creates a downward shockwave.

8. Weird Build Synergies

The upgrade system should deliberately create ridiculous combinations.

For example:

Build A — "Pinball"
Bouncy projectile
Triple shot
Ricochet
Homing
Increased projectile speed

The player essentially turns the house into a pinball machine.

Build B — "The Yeet Machine"
+100% shove force
Faster reload
Increased projectile size
Wall Bonk
Chain Reaction

One hit can potentially send somebody across the entire backyard.

Build C — "Ball Guy"
Throwable objects deal more knockback
Object size increased
Objects bounce
Objects become explosive
Objects return to player

Now the player barely even uses their gun.

They're just throwing furniture.

9. Physics-Based Health

Rather than relying exclusively on traditional health bars, the game should use a combination of:

Health + Impact + Arena Hazards

Players can survive direct hits, but eventually they can be:

Knocked into hazards
Knocked outside the arena
Thrown into dangerous environmental objects
Launched into pits
Knocked through destructible barriers

This makes positioning extremely important.

A player standing near the edge should feel nervous.

10. Arena Hazards

The arena can contain temporary or permanent hazards.

Examples:

Pool

Falling into the pool temporarily removes the player from combat.

Trampoline

Players bounce extremely high.

Garage Door

Can open and close during combat.

Lawn Mower

Moves around the yard as an environmental hazard.

Sprinklers

Create slippery surfaces.

Explosive Grill

Occasionally becomes an extremely bad place to stand.

Breakable Windows

Players can be launched through them.

11. Destruction

The environment should progressively deteriorate during a match.

Early round:

Nice house.

Late round:

What the hell happened here?

Possible destruction:

Furniture gets knocked over
Props scatter
Doors get damaged
Windows break
Objects pile up
Outdoor furniture gets launched everywhere

This gives the match a visual progression.

12. Proximity Voice

The existing proximity voice system becomes an important social mechanic.

Players can hear nearby opponents.

This creates moments like:

"Don't come in this room."

BANG

"OH SHIT."

or:

"I have absolutely no idea what this build does."

three seconds later

YEET

Voice distance should change depending on the environment.

Walls and doors can muffle voices.

13. Multiplayer Modes
Free For All

2–8 players.

Last player standing wins.

This should be the default mode.

Teams

2v2
3v3
4v4

Teams share victory.

Chaos Mode

Every round introduces an environmental modifier.

Examples:

Low gravity
Giant heads
Slippery floor
Everything bounces
Tiny arena
Huge projectiles
Random explosions
Doors move extremely fast
King of the Yard

One player becomes the "King."

Everyone else attempts to knock them out.

The king receives a powerful build bonus.

14. Match Progression

A typical match might look like:

Round 1

Basic weapons.

Simple physics brawl.

↓

Round 2

First upgrades.

Players begin specializing.

↓

Round 3

More powerful builds.

The arena starts getting destroyed.

↓

Round 4

Build combinations become ridiculous.

Physics chaos.

↓

Round 5

Major upgrades.

The house is barely recognizable.

↓

Final Round

All remaining players fight with their accumulated builds.

Maximum chaos.

15. Important Design Principle

The game should not become a traditional gun-based shooter.

The core fantasy is:

"I found a stupid combination of physics and upgrades, and now I can launch you through the neighbor's fence."

The physics simulation should remain the star.

The weapon system exists to manipulate the physics.

The upgrade system exists to make those physics increasingly ridiculous.

16. Visual Style

The game should use a stylized, slightly exaggerated 3D aesthetic.

Think:

Clean shapes
Bright readable environments
Exaggerated physics
Comedic animations
Large impact effects
Strong hit feedback

Avoid overly realistic military aesthetics.

The game should feel like a playground where somebody accidentally installed weapons.

17. Sound Design

Sound should heavily emphasize impact.

Important sounds include:

Weapon firing
Heavy impacts
Objects colliding
Players hitting walls
Doors slamming
Glass breaking
Balls bouncing
Explosions
Comedic player reactions

The stronger the physics interaction, the bigger the sound should feel.

18. Meta Progression

To keep players coming back, matches can unlock:

Player skins
Weapon skins
Projectile effects
Emotes
Voice effects
Hats
House decorations
Victory animations

Avoid making permanent progression increase combat power.

The match itself should determine power.

That keeps games competitive.

19. Technical Design Philosophy

The existing multiplayer architecture is actually an excellent foundation.

The authoritative server should control:

Player movement
Physics
Projectile spawning
Collision
Knockback
Object interaction
Damage
Round state
Upgrade effects
Victory conditions

Clients should primarily handle:

Input
Camera
Visual effects
UI
Audio
Local prediction where appropriate

This is particularly important because physics-based multiplayer becomes extremely frustrating if clients disagree about where objects are.

The Core Fantasy

The elevator pitch should ultimately be:

Yard Brawl is a physics-based multiplayer brawler where 2–8 players fight through a destructible house and backyard, grabbing ridiculous upgrades between rounds and turning simple shove guns into completely broken weapons.

Or, in Steam-store language:

Grab your friends, pick an upgrade, and start throwing each other through the walls. Yard Brawl is a chaotic 2–8 player physics arena brawler where every round makes your weapons more ridiculous, your builds more broken, and the house progressively more fucked.
