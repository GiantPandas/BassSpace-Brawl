# Original assets

`blender/yellow_visor_mascot.blend` is the editable source for the Frank-style
mascot (bare rounded head with a barrel nose and half-lidded eyes, rolled collar,
crossed chest straps and a chest pouch, belt with a brass buckle, sleeves with
cuffs, chunky boots), generated by `tools/generate-assets.py` at 4,492 triangles,
low-poly shaped after `reference/Simplefrank.png` (with `reference/Frank.png` and
`reference/LowPolyFrank.png` for the face and silhouette history).
`public/models/yellow_visor_mascot.glb` contains the rig and Idle, Walk, Sprint,
Crouch, Jump, and Hold animation actions. All original models, code, and
synthesized sound assets in this repository are MIT
licensed.

Regenerate the model with Blender 5.2:

    blender --background --factory-startup --python tools/generate-assets.py

Use the `tools/preview-character.py` companion to render the eleven reference
views (front/back/left/right/three-quarter, head top, face and hood close-ups,
strap-pouch and boots close-ups, wireframe) into versioned folders under
`artifacts/character-previews/vN/`; never delete a version.

The script uses Blender's Python API; Blender is not a runtime dependency.
It clears Blender's saved file-browser directory before writing the `.blend`,
so the committed source does not expose the generating user's home path.
The armature has fourteen bones with rigid weights, including knees, ankles
and elbows. Crouch lowers the hips and bends the legs while preserving body
proportions and keeping the soles level. Walk and sprint use contact, down,
passing, and up poses with knee flex, ankle roll, body rise, weight shift, and
torso counter-motion; runtime playback follows horizontal movement speed.

The materials are the recolor contract the room depends on: `BodyColor / tintable`
covers the head shell, eyelids, hands, and the boots' bright panels — the shoe's
accents are the player's colour, like the head and jacket — and takes the
per-player palette color; `Jacket / tintable` covers the collared jacket and also
tints per player; `Nose / tintable` derives from the body color; `Trim`,
`Buckle / brass`, `EyeWhite`, `EyePupil`, and `Trousers` keep their designed
Blender colors. The room assigns each member an unused palette color and
preserves it through reconnects. The head
ships bare: hats are unlockable cosmetics added by `scene.ts` at runtime, not
GLB geometry; the straps, pouch, collar roll, belt, buckles and cuffs are
original modeled geometry generated by the script. There is no mouth geometry:
a strip laid on the sphere head read as a line floating over the face, so the
face carries eyes, pupils, lids, and the nose only.

The head is bisected at `z 1.46` (`JAW_LINE`) into a `Jawbowl` weighted to
`head` and a `Cranium` weighted to `jaw`; the eyes, brow, nose and ears ride
`jaw` as well, so opening the mouth lifts the whole upper face like a lid about
a hinge near the back-top of the skull (the bone tail sits at `(0, -.10, 1.75)`).
Those two bones carry no keyframes: `GameScene.updatePlayer()`
aims `head` from the speaker's look pitch and `GameScene.render()` opens `jaw`
from their voice level, and animation tracks on either would fight the runtime
for them. Bone names avoid `.` because the glTF exporter strips it.

The head is a rounded cube `0.66` wide x `0.64` deep x `0.68` tall centred at
`(0, -0.02, 1.63)` in Blender space, so its dome top is `z 1.97` and the hat
line sits near `z 1.80`. `Socket_Hat` is an empty parented to the `head` bone at
`(0, -0.02, 1.965)` — on the dome, above the hat line — and is exported as a GLB
node. `scene.ts` finds it with `getObjectByName("Socket_Hat")` and parents every
hat group to it, so a hat rides the head when the head tilts. Hat bases are
authored `0.05-0.10` units below the socket origin so they overlap the skull.
`SkeletonUtils.clone` copies the socket, so every player's avatar carries its own
and the socket survives per-player cloning with no extra code.

`tools/generate-ir.py` creates deterministic original stereo room/hall/yard impulse
responses from filtered noise and early reflection impulses. It has no Python
package dependencies. It also creates the three-second 440 Hz microphone test
tone in `tests/voice-fixture.wav`; no human voice is recorded. Run
`python3 tools/generate-ir.py` to regenerate the WAVs.

The common room and props are original procedural Three.js geometry, with the
collision geometry declared in `src/shared/level.ts`.

Jump and footstep sounds are synthesized in `src/client/audio/spatial.ts`
from oscillators and deterministic noise; no samples or external recordings
are used. `audio/movement.ts` times steps by grounded travel distance, with
quieter crouch steps and a separate jump cue. Clicking the world starts game
audio without requesting microphone access. Other players' movement sounds
use the same spatial emitter and reverb as voice.
